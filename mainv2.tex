\begin{abstract}
Ziemiański's cube chain category $\Ch(K)$ is a combinatorial model for the space of directed paths
on a precubical set $K$. Paliga and Ziemiański built a braid invariant for precubical sets
by constructing an isomorphism between $\pi_1|\Ch(Z)|$ and the braid category, 
where $Z$ is the terminal precubical set.
However, that invariant is trivial for every $K \subseteq \cube n$ \cite[Prop.~6.1]{PZ}.
This paper constructs a localization of $\Ch(K)$ at morphisms that preserve the order
of coordinates, called $\Br(K)$. This structure is the source of the braiding
structure of $\pi_1|\Ch(Z)|$. But, there is no geometric realization or homotopy equivalence in this technique.
Instead we build a 2-polygraph giving an artin braid-like presentation of
$\Br(K)$. The $\{0,1,2\}$-generators of the polygraph are the dimension $\{0,1,2\}$ chains of $K$.
Intuitively, $3$-cells of $K$ contribute braid relations, and pairs of non-intersecting $2$-cells of
$K$ contribute commuting relations. This is not just a metaphor: at $K = Z$,
we get the artin relations and generators exactly. So $\Br(Z)$ is equivalent to the
positive braid category. The presentation is natural in $K$, providing a generalization of the
existing work. As an application, we establish a link to the hyperplane arrangement literature.
The way braids are constructed via salvetti's theorem for the braid arrangement exactly matches
the polygraph we build here for the cube.
All this work is formalized in Lean \cite{Cubical}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Ziemiański's cube chain category $\Ch(K)$ is a combinatorial model for the executions of a
bipointed precubical set $K$ \cite[Thm.~7.6]{Z2}. Paliga and Ziemiański established a homotopy 
equivalence between the space of directed paths on $Z$ and the configuration spaces of points in
the plane \cite[Thm.~1.1]{PZ}, where $Z$ is the terminal precubical set.
It extends, by naturality, to an invariant of any precubical set $K$.
However, it is trivial for every $K \subseteq \cube n$ \cite[Prop.~6.1]{PZ}.
The proof is one line: the invariant factors through the space of directed paths on $\cube n$,
which is contractible.

This paper explains that the braid structure is present in $\Ch(K)$ without passing through 
any homotopy equivalences. We will construct a set of morphisms, $W$, in $\Ch(K)$. 
And define the localization $\Br(K) \;:=\; \Ch(K)\op\bigl[(W\op)\inv\bigr]$. Then we will build a 
2-polygraph, $\Art(K)$, giving an Artin-like presentation for $\Br(K)$ in geometric terms. This approach gives 
us several advantages. Firstly, we preserve more structure of $\Ch(K)$, so $\Br(\cube n)$ does not collapse.
Secondly, it permits us to stay with positive braids which yields a simpler theory.
The full braid group can be easily recovered via the lift to the free groupoid on $\Ch(K)$.

The generators of our polygraph are straightforward. A chain of $K$ has a list of positive integers
for the dimension of the cubes. Let the degree of a chain be the sum of the list minus the length.
Then the polygraph's $n$-generators are the degree $n$ chains, for $n \in \{0,1,2\}$. The attachments
of the 2-generators to paths of 1-generators will take some work to formalize, but the following pictures give 
a clear intuition about the attachments.

The main results of this paper will be
\begin{theorem}[\Cref{thm:A}, \Cref{thm:B}, \Cref{thm:C}]\label{thm:ABC}
  Let $K$ be a bipointed precubical set
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\Art(K)$ presents $\Br(K)$, in the sense that there is an equivalence $E_K : \pres{\Art(K)} \to \Br(K)$.
    \item Both constructions are functorial in $K$, and $E$ is natural: for every $f : K \to L$,
      \[
        E_L \circ \pres{\Art(f)} \;=\; \Br(f) \circ E_K .
      \]
    \item $\Art(Z)$ is isomorphic to the positive Artin polygraph for the braid category.
      Consequently $\Br(Z)$ is equivalent to the positive braid category.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

We are begging the question by naming our polygraph $\Art$, but it is well justified in the spirit of \cite{GGM}. 
At $K = Z$, we see above that we will prove it is indeed the literal Artin presentation of the positive braids.
So in general, we get a subset of the Artin relations in this presentation, depending on the presence of 3-cells of $K$.

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Cube chains.}
A \textbf{shape} is a list $a = [a_1,\dots,a_r]$ of positive integers; it names the serial wedge
$\bigvee a := \cube{a_1} \vee \cdots \vee \cube{a_r}$, whose \textbf{blocks} are the $\cube{a_i}$,
and we put $|a| := \sum_i a_i$ and $\deg a := |a| - r$. Objects of $\Ch(K)$, the \textbf{chains},
are the bipointed maps $d : \bigvee a \to K$, and its morphisms, the refinements, are the maps of
wedges over $K$. Notation defined for shapes, here and below, applies to a chain through its shape:
$|d| := |a|$, $\deg d := \deg a$, and so on. We write $\Ch(u)$ for the pushforward
along $u : K \to L$, which postcomposes the classifying map and leaves the shape alone. A chain of
degree $0$ is a \textbf{run}. The subcategory of runs is discrete; we leave the check to the
reader.

We use two facts from \cite{PZ}, and the third is an easy corollary.

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Ex.~2.6(c)]{PZ}}]\label{lem:wedge-altitude}
  If $a = [a_1,\dots,a_r]$ is a shape, then the unique altitude function on $\bigvee a$ is given
  by $\mathrm{alt}(c) = a_1 + \cdots + a_{i-1} + |c\inv(1)|$ for $c \in \cube{a_i}$.
\end{lemma}

In particular the final vertex of $\bigvee a$ has altitude $|a|$. Moreover $\bigvee a$ is
non-self-linked, by \cite[Prop.~2.10]{PZ} applied to its blocks; we leave the details to the
reader.

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Prop.~2.7]{PZ}}]\label{lem:altitude-preserved}
  Let $P$, $Q$ be connected bipointed precubical sets with altitude functions $\mathrm{alt}_P$ and
  $\mathrm{alt}_Q$. Then every bipointed map $f : P \to Q$ has
  $\mathrm{alt}_Q(f(c)) = \mathrm{alt}_P(c)$ for every cell $c$ of $P$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:concat}
  Let $P, Q \in \BPSet$ with $P \vee Q$ non-self-linked and admitting an altitude. Then
  concatenation $\Ch(P) \times \Ch(Q) \to \Ch(P \vee Q)$ is an isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{The events of a shape.}
The constructions presented in this paper are mostly bookkeeping of coordinates. 
To assist, we're going to define some helpers. First, let $\Ev' : \Cube \to \Set$ be the functor
\begin{enumerate}
  \item sending objects $\cube n$ to its set of directions $[n]$
  \item sending a face embedding $f:\cube n \to \cube m$ to the monotone injection $[n] \to [m]$
    onto the directions that $f$ leaves free.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\Ev$ be the left Kan extension along Yoneda of the $\Ev'$.
Then $\Ev$ is cocontinuous and $\Ev(\cube 0) = \emptyset$, 
so the pushout defining a wedge lifts to a coproduct:
\[
  \Ev\bigl(\textstyle\bigvee a\bigr) \;=\; \coprod_{i \in [r]} [a_i] .
\]
Its elements are the \textbf{events} of $\bigvee a$, the pairs $(i,c)$ with $i \in [r]$ and
$c \in [a_i]$, one for each direction of each block. 
We order the events lexicographically. Numbering them $1, \dots, N$ in this order, where
$N = |a|$, identifies them with $[N]$, and we use whichever description is convenient.
Let $\blk_a(i,c) := i$. 
For a map $f$ of serial wedges we write $f_*$ for $\Ev(f)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:coords}
  Let $f : \bigvee a \to \bigvee b$ in $\BPSet$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item If $g : \bigvee a \to \bigvee b$ in $\BPSet$ has $g_* = f_*$, then $g = f$.
    \item $f_*$ is increasing on each block of $a$.
    \item There is a monotone map $\blk(f)$ from the blocks of $a$ to the blocks of $b$ with
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:blk-coord}
        \blk_b \circ f_* \;=\; \blk(f) \circ \blk_a .
      \end{equation}
    \item $|a| = |b|$, and $f_*$ is a permutation of $[|a|]$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Permutations.}
Write $S_N$ for the group of permutations of $[N]$ and, for $t \in [N-1]$, $s_t := (t,\,t+1)$. For
$i \neq k$ in $[N-1]$, $\cox(i,k)$ is the order of $s_is_k$, which is $3$ when $|i-k| = 1$, $s_is_k$ being a
$3$-cycle, and $2$ otherwise, $s_is_k$ being a product of two disjoint transpositions: these are the entries of the Coxeter matrix of $S_N$, a Coxeter group of type $A_{N-1}$
on the generators $s_t$ \cite[\S1.1, Prop.~1.5.4]{BB}. We read $w \in S_N$ as the list
$w(1), \dots, w(N)$, so that $w$ lists $p$ before $q$ when $w\inv(p) < w\inv(q)$. The
\textbf{left inversion set} of $w$ is
\[
  T_L(w) \;:=\; \bigl\{\, (p,q) \;:\; p < q \text{ and } w\inv(p) > w\inv(q) \,\bigr\} ,
\]
and $t$ is an \textbf{ascent} of $w$ when $w(t) < w(t+1)$. The \textbf{right weak order} on $S_N$
has $u \le w$ when $T_L(u) \subseteq T_L(w)$; it is a partial order by \Cref{lem:weak}(i)
below.\footnote{The formalisation defines the right weak order by lengths adding, as in
\cite[Prop.~3.1.2(ii)]{BB}, and proves that it is this order
(\texttt{WeakOrder.le\_iff\_inversions\_inv\_subset}); \Cref{lem:weak}(iii) is
\texttt{WeakOrder.covBy\_iff}.}

These agree with \cite{BB}, although we use only the definitions above. Reading $(p,q)$ as the
transposition $(p\,q)$, $T_L(w)$ is the set of left associated reflections of $w$
\cite[(1.19)]{BB}, as an easy count of inversions shows; so our order is the right weak order of
\cite[Def.~3.1.1]{BB}, by \cite[Prop.~3.1.3]{BB}. And $t$ is an ascent of $w$ exactly when $s_t$
is not a right descent of $w$ \cite[Prop.~1.5.3]{BB}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weak}
  Let $u, w \in S_N$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $T_L(w)$ determines $w$.
    \item If $t$ is an ascent of $w$, then $T_L(ws_t) = T_L(w) \sqcup \bigl\{(w(t), w(t+1))\bigr\}$.
    \item $w$ covers $u$ if and only if $w = us_t$ for an ascent $t$ of $u$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  (i) $T_L(w)$ says which of any two values $w$ lists first, so it determines the order in which
  $w$ lists all of them, which is $w$.

  (ii) The list $ws_t$ is the list $w$ with its entries in places $t$ and $t+1$ exchanged. So the
  two list every pair of values in the same order except $w(t)$ and $w(t+1)$, which $w$ lists in
  increasing order and $ws_t$ in decreasing order.

  (iii) If $t$ is an ascent of $u$ then $T_L(us_t)$ is $T_L(u)$ with one pair added, by (ii), so
  $us_t$ covers $u$. Conversely let $u < w$. If $w$ listed $u(t)$ and $u(t+1)$ in the same order as
  $u$ for every $t \in [N-1]$, then by transitivity it would list every pair of values in the same
  order as $u$, and $w = u$. So there is a $t$ for which $w$ lists $u(t)$ and $u(t+1)$ in the other
  order. This $t$ is an ascent of $u$, since otherwise $(u(t+1), u(t)) \in T_L(u) \subseteq T_L(w)$
  and $w$ would list the two in the same order as $u$. So $(u(t), u(t+1)) \in T_L(w)$, and (ii)
  gives $T_L(us_t) \subseteq T_L(w)$, that is $u < us_t \le w$. If $w$ covers $u$, then
  $w = us_t$.
\end{proof}

For a shape $a$ on $N$ events put
\[
  \cut(a) \;:=\; \bigl\{\, t \in [N-1] \;:\; \blk_a(t) = \blk_a(t+1) \,\bigr\} , \qquad
  S_a \;:=\; \bigl\{\, w \in S_N \;:\; \blk_a \circ w = \blk_a \,\bigr\} ,
\]
the latter the \textbf{Young subgroup} of $a$ \cite[\S2.4]{BB}; it contains $s_t$ exactly when
$t \in \cut(a)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cuts}
  $a \mapsto \cut(a)$ is a bijection from the shapes on $N$ events onto the subsets of $[N-1]$,
  with $|\cut(a)| = \deg a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  A shape on $N$ events is a composition of $N$ and $\cut(a)$ is the complement of its set of block
  boundaries in $[N-1]$, so this is the classical correspondence \cite[\S1.2]{EC1}, and
  $|\cut(a)| = N - r = \deg a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:young}
  Let $f : \bigvee a \to \bigvee b$ be a map of serial wedges. Then
  $\blk_b \circ f_* = \blk_b = \blk(f) \circ \blk_a$. Hence $f_* \in S_b$ and
  $\cut(a) \subseteq \cut(b)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By \eqref{eq:blk-coord}, $\blk_b \circ f_* = \blk(f) \circ \blk_a$ is monotone, and since $f_*$
  is a bijection its fibre over the $i$-th block of $b$ has $b_i$ elements. A monotone map
  $[N] \to [r]$ is determined by the sizes of its fibres, so it is $\blk_b$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Presented categories.}
There are several equivalent definitions of 2-polygraph, also known as computads.
But the following is a convenient choice for us.
A \textbf{2-polygraph} $\Pg$ consists of a set $\Pg_0$ of \textbf{$0$-generators}, sets
$\Pg_1(x,y)$ of \textbf{$1$-generators} and $\Pg_2(x,y)$ of \textbf{$2$-generators} for
$x, y \in \Pg_0$, and maps
\[
  \src, \tgt : \Pg_2(x,y) \to \Pg_1^*(x,y) ,
\]
where $\Pg_1^*$ is the free category on the $1$-generators, whose morphisms are the
\textbf{paths}. See \cite[Def.~2.2.2]{Polygraphs} for the standard definition; ours is equivalent to it, with
the endpoints of the generators built into the indexing.
The $2$-polygraphs form a category $\Polygraph$ \cite[\S2.2.3]{Polygraphs}. The category
$\pres{\Pg}$ \textbf{presented by} $\Pg$ is the quotient of $\Pg_1^*$ by the smallest congruence
identifying $\src(\alpha)$ with $\tgt(\alpha)$ for every $2$-generator $\alpha$
\cite[\S2.3.3]{Polygraphs}. We write $q:\Pg_1^* \to \pres{\Pg}$ for the quotient functor.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:present-univ}
  Let $\Pg$ be a $2$-polygraph and $C$ a category.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \cite[Lem.~2.1.3]{Polygraphs} Given an object of $C$ for each $0$-generator and an arrow
      of $C$ for each $1$-generator, with matching endpoints, there is a unique functor
      $\Pg_1^* \to C$ extending them.
    \item \cite[Lem.~2.3.4]{Polygraphs} If $F : \Pg_1^* \to C$ is a functor with
      $F(\src\alpha) = F(\tgt\alpha)$ for every $2$-generator $\alpha$, then there is a unique
      functor $\bar F : \pres{\Pg} \to C$ with $\bar F \circ q = F$.
    \item A morphism of $2$-polygraphs bijective on generators in every dimension is an
      isomorphism in $\Polygraph$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pres-functor}
  $\pres{-}$ extends to a functor $\Polygraph \to \Cat$ with $\pres{f} \circ q = q \circ f^*$.
\end{lemma}

\Cref{prop:present-univ}(iii) and \Cref{lem:pres-functor} are routine, and we leave the details to
the reader.

\begin{proposition}[{\cite[\S I.1.1 and Lem.~I.1.2]{GZ}}]\label{prop:loc}
  Let $W$ be a class of morphisms of a category $C$. There is a functor $Q : C \to C[W\inv]$
  inverting $W$. And for every category $D$, $(\cdot) \circ Q$ is an isomorphism from
  $C[W\inv] \to D$ onto the full subcategory of functors $C \to D$ that
  invert $W$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Newman's lemma.}
For a poset $P$, write $x \lessdot y$ when $y$ covers $x$, that is $x < y$ with no $z$ such that $x < z < y$. 
The \textbf{Hasse diagram} $\mathrm{Hasse}(P)$ is the quiver with vertex set $P$ and
one arrow $x \to y$ for each cover $x \lessdot y$. A map of quivers
$\mathrm{Hasse}(P) \to C$ into a category extends to paths by composition, and we read it on paths
without further comment. A functor $F : P \to C$ \textbf{extends} such a map $\varphi$ when
$F(x) = \varphi(x)$ for every $x \in P$ and $F(x \le y) = \varphi(x \lessdot y)$ for every cover
$x \lessdot y$.

The following is a variant of Newman's lemma \cite[Lem.~1.3.21]{Polygraphs}, and its proof is
the same well-founded induction.

\begin{lemma}[Newman's lemma for posets]\label{thm:newman}
  Let $P$ be a finite poset and $\varphi : \mathrm{Hasse}(P) \to C$ a map of quivers. Suppose that
  for every pair of distinct covers $x \lessdot y_1$, $x \lessdot y_2$ and every common upper bound
  $z$ of $y_1$ and $y_2$ there are $j \le z$ and paths $p_i$ from $y_i$ to $j$ with
  \[
  \varphi(p_1) \circ \varphi(x \lessdot y_1) = \varphi(p_2) \circ \varphi(x \lessdot y_2) .
  \]
  Then $\varphi$ extends uniquely to a functor $P \to C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Since $P$ is finite, $x \le z$ holds exactly when there is a path from $x$ to $z$.

  Any two extensions of $\varphi$ agree: if $F$ extends $\varphi$ and $w$ is a path from $x$ to
  $z$, then $x \le z$ is the composite of the covers along $w$, so $F(x \le z) = \varphi(w)$.

  For existence, it suffices to show that $\varphi$ sends parallel paths to equal arrows. Then
  $F(x) := \varphi(x)$ and $F(x \le z) := \varphi(w)$, for any path $w$ from $x$ to $z$, is well
  defined. It is a functor because the empty path gives identities and concatenation gives
  composites, and it extends $\varphi$ because a cover is a path of length one.

  So fix $z \in P$. We show, for every $x \in P$, that any two paths $u$, $v$ from $x$ to $z$ have
  $\varphi(u) = \varphi(v)$, by induction on $x$ downward: assume the claim for every $x' > x$.

  If $x = z$ then $u$ and $v$ are both empty, since a nonempty path from $x$ to $z$ gives
  $x < z$. Otherwise there exist $y_1$ and $y_2$ giving $u = u' \circ (x \lessdot y_1)$ and 
  $v = v' \circ (x \lessdot y_2)$. If $y_1 = y_2$, then
  $\varphi(u') = \varphi(v')$ by induction at $y_1$. Otherwise $z$ is a common upper bound of
  $y_1$ and $y_2$, so the hypothesis gives $j \le z$ and paths $p_i$ from $y_i$ to $j$ with
  \[ 
    \varphi(p_1)\,\varphi(x \lessdot y_1) = \varphi(p_2)\,\varphi(x \lessdot y_2) .
  \]
  Choose a path $r$ from $j$ to $z$, which exists as $j \le z$. 
  Then $u'$ and $r \circ p_1$ are paths from $y_1$ to $z$, so
  $\varphi(u') = \varphi(r)\varphi(p_1)$ by induction at $y_1$, and likewise
  $\varphi(v') = \varphi(r)\varphi(p_2)$. Hence
  \[
    \varphi(u) = \varphi(r)\,\varphi(p_1)\,\varphi(x \lessdot y_1)
               = \varphi(r)\,\varphi(p_2)\,\varphi(x \lessdot y_2) = \varphi(v) .
  \]
\end{proof}


\section{Squares, inversions, and \texorpdfstring{$\Br(K)$}{Br(K)}}\label{sec:squares}

\gap{One paragraph: a square is a pair of events of one block, and the whole construction runs on
the question of which squares a map reverses.}

Throughout, $a$ and $b$ are shapes on $N$ events.

\begin{definition}\label{def:squares}
  Let $\Sq' : \Cube \to \Set$ be the functor
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item sending $\cube n$ to its set of pairs of directions $\{(c,c') \in [n]^2 : c < c'\}$;
    \item sending a face embedding $f : \cube n \to \cube m$ to
      $(c,c') \mapsto (\Ev'(f)c, \Ev'(f)c')$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Let $\Sq$ be the left Kan extension along Yoneda of $\Sq'$. The \textbf{squares} of $a$ are the
  elements of $\Sq(a) := \Sq(\bigvee a)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sq-coords}
  As a set of pairs of events,
  \[
    \Sq(a) \;=\; \bigl\{\, (p,q) \;:\; p < q \text{ and } \blk_a(p) = \blk_a(q) \,\bigr\} ,
  \]
  and a map $f : \bigvee a \to \bigvee b$ of serial wedges has $\Sq(f)(p,q) = (f_*p, f_*q)$. In
  particular $\Sq(f)$ is injective.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  As for $\Ev$, the functor $\Sq$ is cocontinuous and $\Sq(\cube 0) = \emptyset$, so
  $\Sq(\bigvee a) = \coprod_{i \in [r]} \Sq'(\cube{a_i})$, whose element $(i,(c,c'))$ is the pair
  of events $\bigl((i,c),(i,c')\bigr)$.

  The two projections $\Sq' \to \Ev'$, sending $(c,c')$ to $c$ and to $c'$, are natural, so they
  extend to natural transformations $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2 : \Sq \to \Ev$ with
  $\epsilon_1(p,q) = p$ and $\epsilon_2(p,q) = q$. For $k = 1, 2$, naturality at $f$ is the square
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      \Sq(a) \arrow[r, "\Sq(f)"] \arrow[d, "\epsilon_k"'] & \Sq(b) \arrow[d, "\epsilon_k"] \\
      \Ev(\bigvee a) \arrow[r, "f_*"'] & \Ev(\bigvee b)
    \end{tikzcd}
  \]
  and chasing $(p,q)$ around it gives $\Sq(f)(p,q) = (f_*p, f_*q)$. Injectivity follows, since $f_*$ is a
  permutation (\Cref{prop:coords}(iv)).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sq-range}
  Let $f : \bigvee a \to \bigvee b$ be a map of serial wedges. Then $f_*\inv$ preserves the order
  of two events in distinct blocks of $b$. Hence $T_L(f_*) \subseteq \Sq(b)$. A square $(p,q)$ of $b$ lies in $\im\Sq(f)$ if and only if
  $f_*\inv p$ and $f_*\inv q$ lie in one block of $a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Let $u < v$ lie in distinct blocks of $b$, so $\blk_b(u) < \blk_b(v)$, and put $w = f_*\inv u$,
  $w' = f_*\inv v$. Then \eqref{eq:blk-coord} gives $\blk(f)(\blk_a w) < \blk(f)(\blk_a w')$, so
  $\blk_a(w) < \blk_a(w')$ as $\blk(f)$ is monotone, so $w < w'$ as blocks are intervals. An
  element of $T_L(f_*)$ therefore has both events in one block of $b$. For the last claim, a
  square in the image pulls back to a square, whose events share a block; conversely if the two
  preimages share a block then, $f_*$ being increasing there, they form a square mapping to
  $(p,q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cocycle}
  For $f : \bigvee a \to \bigvee b$ and $g : \bigvee b \to \bigvee c$,
  \[
    T_L(g_*f_*) \;=\; \Sq(g)\bigl(T_L(f_*)\bigr) \;\sqcup\; T_L(g_*),
  \]
  and $T_L(\id) = \emptyset$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Let $s$ be a square of $c$. If $s = \Sq(g)(t)$ then $s \notin T_L(g_*)$, the two events of $t$
  sharing a block (\Cref{lem:sq-range}) --- which also gives disjointness --- and
  $(gf)_*\inv(g_*u) = f_*\inv(u)$ for the two events $u$ of $t$, so $s \in T_L(g_*f_*)$ if and
  only if $t \in T_L(f_*)$. If $s \notin \im\Sq(g)$ then $s \notin \Sq(g)(T_L(f_*))$, and its
  two events pull back along $g_*$ into distinct blocks of $b$, so the first claim of
  \Cref{lem:sq-range} applied to $f$ gives $s \in T_L(g_*f_*)$ if and only if $s \in T_L(g_*)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:W}
  A refinement $f$ is \textbf{order preserving} when $f_* = \id$. Write $W = W_K$ for
  the class of order-preserving refinements of $\Ch(K)$, and
  \[
    \Br(K) \;:=\; \Ch(K)\op\bigl[(W\op)\inv\bigr] .
  \]
\end{definition}

Write $Q : \Ch(K)\op \to \Br(K)$ for the localization functor of \Cref{prop:loc}. We leave the
$\op$ on morphisms implicit: for a refinement $u : c \to d$ we write $Q(u) : Q(d) \to Q(c)$, so $Q$
reverses composition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:brop}
  $\Br(K) \simeq \bigl(\Ch(K)[W\inv]\bigr)\op$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  A functor $\Ch(K)\op \to D$ inverting $W\op$ is a functor $\Ch(K) \to D\op$ inverting $W$, and
  conversely. So $\bigl(\Ch(K)[W\inv]\bigr)\op$ has the universal property \Cref{prop:loc} gives
  $\Br(K)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:W}
  A refinement $f$ is order preserving if and only if $T_L(f_*) = \emptyset$. Hence $W$ contains the
  identities; $gf \in W$ if and only if $f \in W$ and $g \in W$; any two order-preserving
  refinements with the same source and target are equal; and $\Ch(u)$ carries $W_K$ into $W_L$, so
  it descends to $\Br(u) : \Br(K) \to \Br(L)$, strictly functorially in $u$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Since $T_L(\id) = \emptyset$, \Cref{lem:weak}(i) gives $T_L(f_*) = \emptyset$ if and only if
  $f_* = \id$.
  The consequences follow: the second from \Cref{lem:cocycle} with $\Sq(g)$ injective (\Cref{lem:sq-coords}), the third
  because two such refinements $f$ and $f'$ have $f_* = \id = f'_*$, so $f = f'$ by
  \Cref{prop:coords}(i), and the last because $\Ch(u)$ changes neither
  shape nor wedge map, with the descent given by \Cref{prop:loc}.
\end{proof}

\section{The Artin polygraph of \texorpdfstring{$K$}{K}}\label{sec:poly}

\gap{Frame the section: the runs over a chain form a poset; its covers are the degree-one chains
over that chain, and the rank-two intervals are the degree-two ones. That is the whole polygraph.}

Fix $K$, and let $d$ be a chain of $K$ on $N$ events.

\subsection{Runs over a chain}

\begin{definition}\label{def:runover}
  A \textbf{run over $d$} is a refinement $x : X \to d$ whose source is a run; write $\RunOver(d)$
  for the set of these.
\end{definition}

Let $d$ have shape $a$. A run $x$ over $d$ is determined by its map of wedges, the classifying map
of its source being forced, and so by $x_*$ (\Cref{prop:coords}(i)). Its source has shape
$[1,\dots,1]$, so $x_*$ sends each time $t \in [N]$ to the event $x$ fires then.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:firing-blocks}
  $x \mapsto x_*$ is a bijection from $\RunOver(d)$ onto $S_a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  It lands in $S_a$ by \Cref{lem:young}, and it is injective by the remark above. An element $w$ of
  $S_a$ fires the events of each block of $d$ during that block's own interval of times, in some
  order. The edge path of $\cube n$ that raises the coordinates in a given order is a bipointed map
  $\bigvee[1,\dots,1] \to \cube n$, and concatenating these over the blocks of $d$ gives a run $x$
  over $d$ with $x_* = w$.
\end{proof}

We order $\RunOver(d)$ by the right weak order: $x \le y$ when $x_* \le y_*$, that is when
$T_L(x_*) \subseteq T_L(y_*)$. Write
$\bot_d$ for the run with $\bot_{d*} = \id$ and $\top_d$ for the run whose permutation reverses
each block of $d$, and call $t$ an \textbf{ascent} of $x$ when it is an ascent of
$x_*$.\footnote{The formalisation \cite{Cubical} orders $\RunOver(d)$ by inclusion of the sets $T_L(x_*)$, which it calls the reversed squares, and
proves that $x \mapsto x_*$ is an order embedding into the right weak order
(\texttt{RunOver.embed}, \texttt{RunOver.weakOrder\_le\_iff}); \Cref{lem:firing-blocks} and
\Cref{lem:lower} are \texttt{RunOver.range\_embed} and \texttt{RunOver.isLowerSet\_range}.}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower}
  $\bot_d$ and $\top_d$ are the least and greatest elements of $\RunOver(d)$, and $S_a$ is a lower
  set of the right weak order on $S_N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{lem:sq-range,lem:sq-coords}, every $x$ has $T_L(x_*) \subseteq \Sq(d)$, the pairs of
  events sharing a block, while $T_L(\bot_{d*}) = \emptyset$ and $T_L(\top_{d*}) = \Sq(d)$.

  Let $w \le x_*$. Then $T_L(w) \subseteq T_L(x_*) \subseteq \Sq(d)$, so $w\inv(p) < w\inv(q)$ whenever $\blk_a(p) < \blk_a(q)$. Hence
  $\blk_a \circ w$ is monotone, and since $w$ is a bijection its fibres have the sizes of those of
  $\blk_a$. So $\blk_a \circ w = \blk_a$, that is $w \in S_a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:push}
  Let $q : o \to d$ be a refinement. Composition with $q$ is an order embedding
  $\RunOver(o) \to \RunOver(d)$, and
  \[
    T_L(q_*x_*) \;=\; \Sq(q)\bigl(T_L(x_*)\bigr) \;\sqcup\; T_L(q_*) .
  \]
  Moreover $q\bot_o = \bot_d$ if and only if $q \in W$; for $x \in \RunOver(d)$ the conditions
  $x \in W$, $T_L(x_*) = \emptyset$ and $x = \bot_d$ agree; $\bot_X = \id_X$ for a run $X$; and
  $\dom\bot_d = \dom\bot_c$ whenever $c \to d$ lies in $W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The formula is \Cref{lem:cocycle}. Since $\Sq(q)$ is injective with image disjoint from $T_L(q_*)$
  (\Cref{lem:sq-coords,lem:sq-range}), $qx \le qy$ if and only if $x \le y$, and
  $T_L((q\bot_o)_*) = T_L(q_*)$. The remaining equivalences are \Cref{def:W,prop:W} and
  \Cref{lem:firing-blocks}, and the last clause is the formula applied to $c \to d$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Covers and cells}

For $x \in \RunOver(d)$ and $t \in \cut(d)$, $s_t$ lies in $S_a$, so there is a unique run
$xs_t$ over $d$ with $(xs_t)_* = x_*s_t$ (\Cref{lem:firing-blocks}). It fires the two events that
$x$ fires at times $t$ and $t+1$ in the other order.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cover}
  The covers of $x$ in $\RunOver(d)$ are the $x \lessdot xs_t$ for the ascents $t \in \cut(d)$ of
  $x$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Since $S_a$ is a lower set (\Cref{lem:lower}), a cover in $\RunOver(d)$ is a cover in $S_N$ with
  both ends in $S_a$. By \Cref{lem:weak}(iii) the covers of $x_*$ in $S_N$ are the $x_*s_t$ for the
  ascents $t$ of $x$, and $x_*s_t \in S_a$ exactly when $s_t \in S_a$, that is $t \in \cut(d)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:factorisations}
  Let $x \in \RunOver(d)$. Sending a factorisation $X \to m \to d$ of $x$ to $\cut(m)$ is a
  bijection onto the subsets of $\cut(d)$. Write $m_K$ for the middle it sends to $K$, and
  $\iota_K : X \to m_K$, $\pi_K : m_K \to d$ for the two legs. Then $\deg m_K = |K|$, and
  $\iota_K$ is order preserving if and only if every element of $K$ is an ascent of $x$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let $x = \pi\iota$ with $\iota : X \to m$ and $\pi : m \to d$, and let $c$ be the shape of $m$.
  Then $\cut(c) \subseteq \cut(d)$ and $\iota_* \in S_c$ (\Cref{lem:young}). So each block $B$ of
  $c$, an interval of $[N]$, is carried by $\iota_*$ onto itself and by $x_* = \pi_*\iota_*$ onto
  $\pi_*(B)$, and $\pi_*$ is increasing on $B$ (\Cref{prop:coords}(ii)).

  The subset $\cut(c)$ determines $c$ (\Cref{lem:cuts}), hence its blocks $B$. On each of them
  $\pi_*$ is the increasing bijection onto $x_*(B)$, so $\pi_*$ is determined, hence so are $\pi$
  (\Cref{prop:coords}(i)) and the classifying map $d\pi$ of $m$. Then $\iota_* = \pi_*\inv x_*$ is
  determined, hence so is $\iota$. Conversely, every $K \subseteq \cut(d)$ arises. TODO: construct
  $\pi$ blockwise from faces of the blocks of $d$, and $\iota$ from it.

  By \Cref{lem:cuts}, $\deg m = |\cut(c)|$. Finally $\iota$ is order preserving exactly when
  $\iota_* = \id$ (\Cref{prop:W}). On each block $B$ of $c$, $x_* = \pi_*\iota_*$ with $\pi_*$
  increasing, so $\iota_*$ is the identity on $B$ exactly when $x_*$ is increasing on $B$, and
  $x_*$ is increasing on every block of $c$ exactly when every element of $\cut(c)$ is an ascent
  of $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:cell}
  For runs $X, Y$ of $K$, an \textbf{$n$-cell} from $X$ to $Y$ is a chain $e$ with
  $\deg e = n$, $\dom\bot_e = X$ and $\dom\top_e = Y$.
\end{definition}

\gap{One sentence: so the $n$-cells are the chains of degree $n$, sorted by the two runs each
spans, and a $0$-cell is a run.}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:closure}
  Let every element of $K \subseteq \cut(d)$ be an ascent of $x \in \RunOver(d)$. Then
  \[
    T_L\bigl((\pi_K \top_{m_K})_*\bigr) \;=\; T_L(x_*) \sqcup \im\Sq(\pi_K),
  \]
  and every $z \ge x$ with $\bigl(x_*(t), x_*(t+1)\bigr) \in T_L(z_*)$ for all $t \in K$
  satisfies $\im\Sq(\pi_K) \subseteq T_L(z_*)$. So $\pi_K\top_{m_K}$ is the least such $z$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Since $T_L(\top_{m_K*}) = \Sq(m_K)$ (\Cref{lem:lower}), \Cref{prop:push} gives
  $T_L((\pi_K\top_{m_K})_*) = \im\Sq(\pi_K) \sqcup T_L(\pi_{K*})$, and \Cref{lem:cocycle} applied to
  $\pi_K\iota_K = x$, with $\iota_K$ order preserving (\Cref{prop:factorisations}), gives
  $T_L(\pi_{K*}) = T_L(x_*)$.

  For the second claim, $\im\Sq(\pi_K)$ is the set of pairs of events inside one of the sets
  $\pi_{K*}(B)$, for $B$ a block of $m_K$ (\Cref{lem:sq-coords}). Since $\iota_{K*} = \id$ we have
  $\pi_{K*} = x_*$. Write $B = \{u, \dots, u+k\}$ and $p_i := x_*(u+i)$; then
  $p_0 < \cdots < p_k$, as the elements of $B \cap \cut(m_K) \subseteq K$ are ascents of $x$, and
  each $(p_i, p_{i+1})$ lies in $T_L(z_*)$. So $z$ fires $p_k, \dots, p_0$ in this order, and every
  pair of them lies in $T_L(z_*)$. The last sentence follows, since $\pi_K\top_{m_K}$ is itself
  such a $z$.
\end{proof}

For a cover $x \lessdot xs_t$ of $\RunOver(d)$ put $\gamma(x \lessdot xs_t) := m_{\{t\}}$, the
middle of \Cref{prop:factorisations} for $x$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:covercell}
  Let $x \lessdot y = xs_t$ in $\RunOver(d)$. Then $\gamma(x \lessdot y)$ is a $1$-cell from
  $\dom x$ to $\dom y$, with $\pi_{\{t\}}\bot_{m_{\{t\}}} = x$ and $\pi_{\{t\}}\top_{m_{\{t\}}} = y$;
  and every $1$-cell $\alpha$ is $\gamma$ of the unique cover $\bot_\alpha \lessdot \top_\alpha$ of
  $\RunOver(\alpha)$. So $\gamma$, together with $x \mapsto \dom x$ on vertices, is a map of quivers
  from $\mathrm{Hasse}(\RunOver(d))$ to the quiver of $1$-cells. Finally, for every refinement
  $q : o \to d$ and every cover $e = (x \lessdot xs_t)$ of $\RunOver(o)$,
  $qe = \bigl(qx \lessdot (qx)s_t\bigr)$ is a cover of $\RunOver(d)$ and $\gamma(qe) = \gamma(e)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Write $m = m_{\{t\}}$, $\pi = \pi_{\{t\}}$ and $\iota = \iota_{\{t\}}$. By \Cref{lem:cover}, $t$ is
  an ascent of $x$, so $\iota$ is order preserving (\Cref{prop:factorisations}), hence equals
  $\bot_m$ (\Cref{prop:push}). So $\pi\bot_m = x$, $\dom\bot_m = \dom x$ and $\deg m = 1$. The one
  block of $m$ of dimension $2$ is $\{t, t+1\}$, so $\top_{m*} = s_t$, and $\pi_* = x_*$; hence
  $(\pi\top_m)_* = x_*s_t$, so $\pi\top_m = y$ and $\dom\top_m = \dom y$.

  Conversely a chain $\alpha$ of degree $1$ has $\cut(\alpha) = \{t\}$ for one $t$, so
  $\RunOver(\alpha)$ is the two-element poset $\bot_\alpha \lessdot \bot_\alpha s_t = \top_\alpha$
  (\Cref{lem:firing-blocks,lem:cover}). The factorisation $\bot_\alpha = \id_\alpha \circ \bot_\alpha$
  has middle $\alpha$, so $\gamma(\bot_\alpha \lessdot \top_\alpha) = \alpha$ by
  \Cref{prop:factorisations}.

  For the last claim, $t \in \cut(o)$ (\Cref{lem:cover}), so $x_*(t)$ and $x_*(t+1)$ share a block
  of $o$, on which $q_*$ is increasing (\Cref{prop:coords}(ii)); so $t$ is an ascent of $qx$, and
  $t \in \cut(d)$ by \Cref{lem:young}. Hence $qx \lessdot (qx)s_t = q(xs_t)$ (\Cref{lem:cover}),
  both having permutation $q_*x_*s_t$. The factorisation $qx = (q\pi_{\{t\}})\,\iota_{\{t\}}$ has
  middle $m_{\{t\}}$, with cut $\{t\}$, so $\gamma(qe) = m_{\{t\}} = \gamma(e)$ by
  \Cref{prop:factorisations}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The attachment of a 2-cell}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:polygon}
  Let $\deg e = 2$ and $\cut(e) = \{i < k\}$. Then $|\Sq(e)| = \cox(i,k)$: either $k = i+1$ and
  $e$ has one block of dimension $3$, or $k > i+1$ and $e$ has two blocks of dimension $2$. The
  poset $\RunOver(e)$ has exactly two maximal chains,
  \[
    \bot_e \lessdot \bot_es_i \lessdot \bot_es_is_k \lessdot \cdots
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \bot_e \lessdot \bot_es_k \lessdot \bot_es_ks_i \lessdot \cdots,
  \]
  each of length $\cox(i,k)$ and ending at $\top_e$. Write $\src(e)$ and $\tgt(e)$ for the paths
  of $1$-cells that $\gamma$ reads off the first and the second.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The positions $i, i+1$ and $k, k+1$ share blocks, and no others do. So if $k = i+1$ then
  $i, i+1, i+2$ form one block of dimension $3$, with $\binom32 = 3$ squares, and otherwise
  $\{i,i+1\}$ and $\{k,k+1\}$ are two blocks of dimension $2$, with $2$ squares
  (\Cref{lem:sq-coords}).

  By \Cref{lem:cover}, every $u \in \RunOver(e)$ has exactly two neighbours in
  $\mathrm{Hasse}(\RunOver(e))$, namely $us_i$ and $us_k$: if $t$ is an ascent of $u$ then
  $u \lessdot us_t$, and otherwise $t$ is an ascent of $us_t$ and $us_t \lessdot u$. The poset is
  finite with a least element, so its Hasse diagram is connected, hence a cycle, on the
  $2\cox(i,k)$ elements of $S_e$ (\Cref{lem:firing-blocks}). Along a cover $u \lessdot us_t$ the
  size of $T_L(u_*)$ rises by one (\Cref{lem:weak}(ii)), and $T_L(\bot_{e*}) = \emptyset$ while
  $|T_L(\top_{e*})| = |\Sq(e)| = \cox(i,k)$ (\Cref{lem:lower}). So each of the
  two paths from $\bot_e$ to $\top_e$ round the cycle rises at every step, and these are the two
  maximal chains. They leave $\bot_e$ through $\bot_es_i$ and $\bot_es_k$, and the letters
  alternate because the two edges at each vertex are labelled $s_i$ and $s_k$.
\end{proof}

In both cases $\src(e)$ reverses the squares of $e$ in lexicographic order and $\tgt(e)$ in the
reverse order, as a direct check shows.\footnote{The formalisation \cite{Cubical} works with the
sets $T_L(x_*)$ in place of the permutations $x_*$: \texttt{reversedIso} identifies $\RunOver(d)$
with the subsets of $\Sq(d)$ that are \emph{biclosed}, meaning that no block's induced tournament
has a directed $3$-cycle, and it takes the last sentence above as the definition of the two paths.
The four and the six runs are \texttt{card\_biclosed\_of\_isEmpty\_triangles} and
\texttt{card\_biclosed\_of\_triangle}, and this proposition is derived twice there: as
\texttt{eq\_srcPath\_or\_tgtPath\_of\_triangles}, from \texttt{subsingleton\_triangles} and
\texttt{biclosed\_iff\_of\_triangle}, and as \texttt{eq\_srcPath\_or\_tgtPath}, from
$2$-regularity of $\mathrm{Hasse}(\RunOver(e))$.}

The two species are drawn in \Cref{fig:species-sq} and \Cref{fig:species-cube}.
\begin{definition}\label{def:poly}
  The \textbf{Artin polygraph} $\Art(K)$ has the runs of $K$ as $0$-generators. Its
  $1$-generators and $2$-generators from $X$ to $Y$ are the $1$-cells and $2$-cells from $X$ to $Y$
  of \Cref{def:cell}, that is the chains of degree $1$ and $2$ spanning those two runs. For a
  $2$-generator $e$, $\src(e)$ and $\tgt(e)$ are the paths of \Cref{prop:polygon}.
\end{definition}

\gap{One paragraph on the name: $\Art(K)$ is built from $K$ alone, with no braid theory in it, and
\Cref{prop:polygon} already exhibits its $2$-generators as braid and commutation relations;
\Cref{thm:C} is what makes the name literal.}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:closes}
  Read in $\pres{\Art(K)}$, the map of quivers $\gamma$ satisfies the hypothesis of
  \Cref{thm:newman} on $\RunOver(d)$. So it extends uniquely to a functor
  $\Gamma_d : \RunOver(d) \to \pres{\Art(K)}$, and $\Gamma_d(qx) = \Gamma_o(x)$ for every
  refinement $q : o \to d$ and every $x \in \RunOver(o)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  Let $x \lessdot y_1 = xs_{t_1}$ and $x \lessdot y_2 = xs_{t_2}$ be distinct covers, and $z$ a
  common upper bound. Then $t_1 \neq t_2$ are ascents of $x$ in $\cut(d)$ (\Cref{lem:cover}), so for
  $K = \{t_1, t_2\}$ the middle $m_K$ is a chain of degree $2$, that is a $2$-cell
  (\Cref{prop:factorisations}). Each $y_i$ fires $x_*(t_i+1)$ before $x_*(t_i)$, so
  $\bigl(x_*(t_i), x_*(t_i+1)\bigr) \in T_L(y_{i*}) \subseteq T_L(z_*)$, and
  $j := \pi_K\top_{m_K}$ satisfies $j \le z$ (\Cref{lem:closure}).

  Since $\iota_K$ is order preserving it equals $\bot_{m_K}$ (\Cref{prop:factorisations},
  \Cref{prop:push}), so composition with $\pi_K$ carries $\bot_{m_K}$ to $x$, $\top_{m_K}$ to $j$
  and $\bot_{m_K}s_{t_i}$ to $xs_{t_i} = y_i$, and it carries covers to covers and keeps the
  $1$-cell each cover reads as (\Cref{cor:covercell}). So the two maximal chains of
  $\RunOver(m_K)$ (\Cref{prop:polygon}) become two paths from $x$ to $j$, leaving by $y_1$ and by
  $y_2$ and reading as $\src(m_K)$ and $\tgt(m_K)$, which are equal in $\pres{\Art(K)}$.

  For the last claim, composition with $q$ is monotone, so $x \mapsto \Gamma_d(qx)$ and $\Gamma_o$
  are functors out of $\RunOver(o)$. Both extend $\gamma$ on $\RunOver(o)$, because composition
  with $q$ keeps domains, carries covers to covers and keeps $\gamma$ (\Cref{cor:covercell}). So
  they are equal, by the uniqueness in \Cref{thm:newman}.
\end{proof}

\section{The presentation}\label{sec:thmA}

\gap{Frame the proof as a comparison of two universal properties: a functor out of
$\pres{\Art(K)}$ is an arrow for each degree-one chain identifying $\src(e)$ and $\tgt(e)$ for every
degree-two chain $e$ (\Cref{prop:present-univ}), and a functor out of $\Br(K)$ is a functor on
$\Ch(K)\op$ inverting $W$ (\Cref{prop:loc}). Each is built from the other.}

Fix $K$ throughout this section, and write $E$, $\Theta$, $\Phi$ for the functors called $E_K$,
$\Theta_K$, $\Phi_K$ elsewhere.

\gap{One sentence: every arrow of $\Br(K)$ written down below is a composite $Q(g)Q(m)\inv$, and

An $n$-cell $\alpha$ from $X$ to $Y$ comes with the two refinements $\bot_\alpha : X \to \alpha$ and
$\top_\alpha : Y \to \alpha$, of which the first is order preserving; since $Q$ inverts
$\bot_\alpha$, we may set
\[
  E(X) := Q(X), \qquad
  E(\alpha) \;:=\; Q(\top_\alpha) \circ Q(\bot_\alpha)\inv \;:\; Q(X) \to Q(Y) .
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:telescope}
  Let $d$ be a chain and $z \in \RunOver(d)$. Every path from $\bot_d$ to $z$ in
  $\mathrm{Hasse}(\RunOver(d))$ reads, under $E \circ \gamma$, as
  $Q(z) \circ Q(\bot_d)\inv$. Consequently $E$ extends to a functor
  $\pres{\Art(K)} \to \Br(K)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  Induct on the length of the path, the empty path reading as the identity. For a cover
  $x \lessdot y = xs_t$, put $\beta := \gamma(x \lessdot y)$ and $q := \pi_{\{t\}}$, so
  $x = q\bot_\beta$ and $y = q\top_\beta$ (\Cref{cor:covercell}), so $Q(x) = Q(\bot_\beta)Q(q)$.
  The middle two factors of
  \[
    E(\beta) \circ Q(x) \circ Q(\bot_d)\inv
      \;=\; Q(\top_\beta) \circ Q(\bot_\beta)\inv \circ Q(\bot_\beta) \circ Q(q)
            \circ Q(\bot_d)\inv
  \]
  cancel, leaving $Q(\top_\beta)Q(q)Q(\bot_d)\inv = Q(y)Q(\bot_d)\inv$.

  For the extension, \Cref{prop:present-univ}(i) extends $E$ to a functor
  $\Art(K)_1^* \to \Br(K)$. For a $2$-generator $e$, $\src(e)$ and $\tgt(e)$ are both paths from
  $\bot_e$ to $\top_e$ in $\mathrm{Hasse}(\RunOver(e))$ (\Cref{prop:polygon}), so they have equal
  image, and \Cref{prop:present-univ}(ii) gives the functor on $\pres{\Art(K)}$.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}\label{lem:theta}
  Setting $\Theta(d) := \dom\bot_d$ and, for a refinement $u : c \to d$,
  \[
    \Theta(u) \;:=\; \Gamma_d\bigl(\bot_d \le u\bot_c\bigr)
      \;:\; \dom\bot_d \to \dom\bot_c ,
  \]
  defines a functor $\Theta : \Ch(K)\op \to \pres{\Art(K)}$; it sends $u$ to an identity whenever
  $u$ is order preserving, so it descends uniquely to $\Phi : \Br(K) \to \pres{\Art(K)}$ with
  $\Phi Q = \Theta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The stated target is the right one: $u\bot_c$ lies in $\RunOver(d)$ with source $\dom\bot_c$.
  Let $v : b \to c$ and $u : c \to d$. Composition with $u$ is monotone (\Cref{prop:push}), so
  $\bot_d \le u\bot_c \le uv\bot_b$, and $\Gamma_d$ being a functor,
  $\Theta(uv) = \Gamma_d(u\bot_c \le uv\bot_b) \circ \Theta(u)$; the left factor is
  $\Gamma_c(\bot_c \le v\bot_b) = \Theta(v)$ by the last clause of \Cref{prop:closes}. If $u$
  is order preserving then $u\bot_c = \bot_d$ (\Cref{prop:push}), so the comparison is an identity.
  The descent is \Cref{prop:loc}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:A}
  For every bipointed precubical set $K$, the functor $E$ is an equivalence
  $\pres{\Art(K)} \to \Br(K)$, with $\Phi$ inverse to it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  On a $0$-cell, $\Phi(E(X)) = \Theta(X) = \dom\bot_X = X$ (\Cref{prop:push}). On a $1$-cell
  $\alpha$, applying $\Phi$ to the defining composite gives
  $\Phi(E(\alpha)) = \Theta(\top_\alpha) \circ \Theta(\bot_\alpha)\inv$, whose second
  factor is an identity because $\bot_\alpha$ is order preserving (\Cref{lem:theta}). Since
  $\top_\alpha\bot_Y = \top_\alpha$ and $\RunOver(\alpha)$ is the two-element poset
  $\bot_\alpha \lessdot \top_\alpha$,
  \[
    \Theta(\top_\alpha) \;=\; \Gamma_\alpha(\bot_\alpha \le \top_\alpha)
      \;=\; q\bigl(\gamma(\bot_\alpha \lessdot \top_\alpha)\bigr) \;=\; q(\alpha)
  \]
  by \Cref{cor:covercell}. So $\Phi E q$ and $q$ agree on generators, and $\Phi E = \id$ by
  the uniqueness in \Cref{prop:present-univ}(i) and (ii).

  For the other composite, \Cref{lem:telescope} gives
  $E(\Theta(u)) = Q(u\bot_c)Q(\bot_d)\inv
   = Q(\bot_c) \circ Q(u) \circ Q(\bot_d)\inv$, which is $Q(u)$ conjugated by the isomorphisms
  $Q(\bot_c)$ and $Q(\bot_d)$. So the $Q(\bot_d)$ form a natural isomorphism $Q \cong E\Theta = E\Phi Q$,
  and since precomposition with $Q$ is an isomorphism onto a full subcategory (\Cref{prop:loc}),
  it is $\eta Q$ for a unique natural transformation $\eta : \id \Rightarrow E\Phi$, which is
  invertible because $\eta Q$ is.
\end{proof}

\gap{One sentence: the comparison is an equivalence and not an isomorphism because $\Br(K)$ is not
skeletal --- a chain and the run below it become isomorphic there without being equal --- so the
runs are a skeleton of $\Br(K)$.}

\section{Naturality}\label{sec:thmB}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:B}
  Sending $K$ to $\Art(K)$, and $f : K \to L$ to $\Ch(f)$ on generators in every dimension, is a functor
  $\BPSet \to \Polygraph$; and for every $f : K \to L$,
  \[
    E_L \circ \pres{\Art(f)} \;=\; \Br(f) \circ E_K
    \;:\; \pres{\Art(K)} \longrightarrow \Br(L) .
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  A run over a chain $e$ is determined by its map of wedges
  (\Cref{def:runover}), and $\Ch(f) e$ has the same shape and the same maps of wedges into it,
  so $\RunOver(e) = \RunOver(\Ch(f) e)$ as ordered sets, with the same permutations $x_*$.
  The factorisations of \Cref{prop:factorisations} and $\gamma$ (\Cref{cor:covercell}) are read off
  the maps of wedges and the shapes alone, so $\Ch(f)$ preserves $\bot$, $\top$, cells, covers and
  $\gamma$; and $\cut(\Ch(f) e) = \cut(e)$, so it preserves $\src$ and $\tgt$ letter by letter
  (\Cref{prop:polygon}). Hence $\Art(f)$ is a morphism of $2$-polygraphs, functorially in
  $f$ because $\Ch$ is a functor.

  Both composites send a run $X$ to $Q(\Ch(f)X)$. Let $\alpha$ be a $1$-generator of $\Art(K)$.
  By \Cref{lem:pres-functor}, $E_L \circ \pres{\Art(f)}$ sends $q(\alpha)$ to
  $E_L(\Ch(f)\alpha)$. On the other side, $\Br(f)$ is the
  descent of the functor induced by $\Ch(f)$, so it sends $E_K(\alpha)$ to
  $Q(\Ch(f)\top_\alpha) \circ Q(\Ch(f)\bot_\alpha)\inv$. Here $\Ch(f)\bot_\alpha$
  is an order-preserving refinement into $\Ch(f)\alpha$ out of a run, hence is
  $\bot_{\Ch(f)\alpha}$ (\Cref{prop:push}), and
  $\Ch(f)\top_\alpha = \top_{\Ch(f)\alpha}$ because both reverse every square. So $\Br(f) \circ E_K$ also sends $q(\alpha)$ to
  $E_L(\Ch(f)\alpha)$. Precomposed with $q$, the two composites agree on generators, so they
  are equal by the uniqueness in \Cref{prop:present-univ}(i) and (ii).
\end{proof}

\gap{One sentence: the square is an equality, not an isomorphism, because an order-preserving
refinement is determined by its source and target (\Cref{prop:W}), so transporting the two legs of
a cell involves no choice. The tree states the equality, \texttt{paperSquare}, and derives the
comparison isomorphism from it as \texttt{eqToIso}.}

\section{The point}\label{sec:thmC}

Since $Z$ is terminal, every serial wedge has exactly one bipointed map to it and every map of
wedges commutes over it, so $\Ch(Z)$ is the category of shapes and maps of wedges. Write $Z_N$ for
the run of $Z$ on $N$ events. Both $\bot_e$ and $\top_e$ have source a run on $|e|$ events, so
every cell of $Z$ is a loop at $Z_{|e|}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:zcells}
  Sending a chain to its cut set is a bijection from the $n$-cells at $Z_N$ onto the $n$-element
  subsets of $[N-1]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  A chain of $Z$ is determined by its shape, every chain on $N$ events is a cell at $Z_N$, and
  \Cref{lem:cuts} identifies the shapes of degree $n$ on $N$ events with the $n$-element subsets
  of $[N-1]$.
\end{proof}

Write $\sigma_k$ for the $1$-cell at $Z_N$ with $\cut = \{k\}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:alternate}
  Let $e$ be a chain of $Z$ with $\deg e = 2$ and $\cut(e) = \{i<k\}$. Then
  \[
    \src(e) = \sigma_i\sigma_k\sigma_i\cdots, \qquad \tgt(e) = \sigma_k\sigma_i\sigma_k\cdots,
  \]
  both of length $\cox(i,k)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  A cover $u \lessdot us_t$ of $\RunOver(e)$ reads as $\gamma(u \lessdot us_t) = m_{\{t\}}$, a
  $1$-cell at $Z_N$ with cut $\{t\}$ (\Cref{prop:factorisations}), which is $\sigma_t$
  (\Cref{lem:zcells}). So this is \Cref{prop:polygon}, read letter by letter.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:artin-poly}
  \textbf{Artin's braid polygraph} $\ArtBraid$ has one $0$-generator for each $N \in \N$; at $N$,
  the $1$-generators $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{N-1}$, each a loop; and, for each pair $i<k$ in
  $[N-1]$, one $2$-generator $e$ with $\src(e) = \sigma_i\sigma_k\sigma_i\cdots$ and
  $\tgt(e) = \sigma_k\sigma_i\sigma_k\cdots$, both of length $\cox(i,k)$.
\end{definition}

\gap{One or two sentences: unwind \Cref{def:artin-poly} to Artin's relations \cite{Artin}, so
$\pres{\ArtBraid}$ has endomorphism monoid $B_N^+$ at $N$ and no arrows between distinct objects.}

Write $\BraidPos$ for the category with objects $N \in \N$, no arrows between distinct objects, and
$\End(N) = B_N^+$ composed left to right, as we read words.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:C}
  $\Art(Z) \cong \ArtBraid$ in $\Polygraph$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  The runs of $Z$ are the $Z_N$ and every cell of $Z$ is a loop, so both polygraphs are disjoint
  unions over $N$ and it suffices to compare at each $N$. There the $1$-generators are indexed by
  $[N-1]$ and the $2$-generators by the pairs $i<k$ (\Cref{lem:zcells}), and by \Cref{prop:alternate}
  $\src$ and $\tgt$ of the $2$-generator at $\{i<k\}$ are those of \Cref{def:artin-poly}. The
  resulting morphism of $2$-polygraphs is bijective on generators in every dimension, hence an isomorphism
  (\Cref{prop:present-univ}(iii)).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:brZ}
  $\Br(Z) \simeq \BraidPos$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  By \Cref{thm:A} at $K = Z$, $\Br(Z) \simeq \pres{\Art(Z)}$, and by \Cref{thm:C} and
  \Cref{lem:pres-functor}, $\pres{\Art(Z)} \cong \pres{\ArtBraid}$. TODO: $\pres{\ArtBraid} \cong
  \BraidPos$, which is the unwinding promised after \Cref{def:artin-poly}, with the left-to-right
  composition convention.
\end{proof}
